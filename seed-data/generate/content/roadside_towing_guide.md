# Roadside Assistance and Towing Coverage Guide

**Document ID:** MMIC-ROADSIDE-2025
**Audience:** Claims adjusters
**Effective:** June 1, 2025

*This guide expands on the Roadside Assistance endorsement referenced in the Policy Form Version Comparison with claims-handling detail for towing and disablement events.*

## 1. What Roadside Assistance Covers

Roadside assistance, where purchased and shown on the Declarations page, pays for towing to the nearest qualified repair facility and limited on-site services (jump-start, flat-tire change, lockout assistance, fuel delivery) following a mechanical breakdown or disablement of the covered auto. It is a service benefit distinct from Part D or Part C physical damage coverage and is payable regardless of whether the disablement resulted from a covered accident.

## 2. Distinguishing Breakdown Towing from Accident Towing

Towing following a mechanical breakdown is adjudicated under the Roadside Assistance endorsement described here. Towing following a covered accident (a collision or comprehensive loss) is instead a component of the Part D or Part C claim itself, since it is an expense directly caused by the covered loss rather than an independent breakdown — these two towing scenarios use different coverage citations and should not be conflated in the claim file even though the service performed (towing a vehicle) looks identical from the policyholder's perspective.

## 3. Per-Occurrence Limit

Roadside assistance is subject to a per-occurrence dollar limit shown on the Declarations page; a tow exceeding that limit (for example, a long-distance tow from a remote location) is paid up to the limit, with the excess the policyholder's own responsibility, unless the tow qualifies instead as an accident-related tow under Section 2, which is evaluated against the Part D or Part C limit rather than the roadside-assistance limit.

## 4. Frequency Limits

Some Roadside Assistance selections carry an annual frequency limit (a maximum number of service calls per policy period) in addition to the per-occurrence dollar limit; both limits are checked independently, since a policyholder could be within the dollar limit on an individual call but have already exhausted the annual frequency limit.

## 5. Vehicles Not Covered

Roadside assistance applies only to a covered auto listed on the Declarations page — a non-owned or borrowed vehicle the policyholder is driving is not covered under the policyholder's own Roadside Assistance selection, consistent with the general non-owned-vehicle limitation described elsewhere in this corpus for other coverage parts.

## 6. Repeated Breakdowns and Mechanical Condition

A pattern of repeated roadside-assistance calls for the same vehicle within a short period may indicate an underlying mechanical condition rather than isolated incidents; this is a maintenance issue for the policyholder to address and is not itself a basis for coverage denial of any individual call, but is documented and monitored consistent with the pattern-tracking approach used elsewhere in this corpus (Vendor and Repair Network Management Guide, Section 7).

## 7. Reimbursement Where No Network Provider Is Available

Where no network towing provider is available (a remote location, after-hours breakdown), the policyholder may arrange towing independently and submit for reimbursement up to the applicable limit; the reimbursement is calculated by the deterministic calculation service against the documented invoice and the Declarations-page limit, following the same computation discipline used throughout this corpus.

## 8. Documentation Standard

A roadside assistance claim file documents: the disablement cause (to distinguish Section 1 breakdown towing from Section 2 accident towing), the service performed, the invoice or network provider confirmation, and the applicable per-occurrence and frequency limits from the Declarations page.
