# Multi-Vehicle Policy and Household Structure Guide

**Document ID:** MMIC-MULTIVEHICLE-2025
**Audience:** Underwriting and claims adjusters
**Effective:** June 1, 2025

*This guide expands on the Underwriting Guidelines with the specific rules for policies covering more than one vehicle or more than one driver in a household.*

## 1. Multi-Vehicle Declarations Structure

A multi-vehicle policy lists each covered auto separately on the Declarations page, each with its own set of selected coverages, limits, and deductibles — coverage selections are not assumed to be identical across vehicles on the same policy. An adjuster handling a claim on a multi-vehicle policy confirms the specific vehicle involved and reads that vehicle's own line on the Declarations page, rather than assuming the policy's coverage applies uniformly.

## 2. Multi-Car Discount

A multi-vehicle policy typically receives a rating discount reflected in the premium calculation described in the Personal Auto Rating and Rules Manual; this discount is a pricing matter and has no effect on claims adjudication — a claim is evaluated purely against the specific vehicle's own coverage selections per Section 1, independent of how the premium was calculated.

## 3. Household Drivers Not Listed as Named Insureds

A household member who regularly drives a covered auto but is not listed as a named insured on the Declarations page is generally still covered while driving a listed covered auto with permission, under the permissive-use provisions of Part A, subject to any household-resident-driver disclosure requirement in the Underwriting Guidelines. A driver who should have been disclosed at underwriting but was not is reviewed against the material-misrepresentation provisions referenced in the Underwriting Guidelines before a claim involving that driver is finalized.

## 4. Excluded Driver Endorsement

Where a household member has been specifically excluded from coverage via an excluded-driver endorsement (typically because that individual does not qualify for coverage or has a driving record the policyholder elected to exclude from rating), a claim arising while that specific excluded individual was driving is denied under Part A regardless of vehicle ownership, since the exclusion applies to the driver by name, not to a specific vehicle.

## 5. Vehicle-Specific Versus Policy-Wide Endorsements

Some endorsements apply to a specific listed vehicle (for example, a New Vehicle Replacement Cost endorsement tied to one particular new car) while others apply policy-wide (for example, Roadside Assistance, which typically covers whichever covered auto is disabled). The Declarations page indicates which vehicles a given endorsement applies to; an adjuster does not assume a vehicle-specific endorsement extends automatically to every vehicle on a multi-vehicle policy.

## 6. Adding or Removing a Vehicle Mid-Term

A vehicle added mid-term is covered from the effective date of the change endorsement, using whichever Policy Form version governs the policy as of that date — not necessarily the same form version under which the original policy was written, if a renewal and form version change occurred between original issuance and the mid-term vehicle addition. A vehicle removed mid-term has no coverage for any loss occurring after the removal's effective date, even if the loss is reported later.

## 7. Claims Involving Multiple Vehicles on the Same Policy in One Accident

Where two vehicles on the same multi-vehicle policy are both involved in the same accident (for example, a household member driving one covered vehicle collides with another covered vehicle also on the policy), each vehicle's damage is adjudicated under its own Part D or Part C coverage and deductible independently; Part A liability coverage generally does not apply between two vehicles insured under the same policy and the same named insured, and this scenario is reviewed against the Policy Wording's specific provisions for intra-household vehicle accidents before any liability determination is made.

## 8. Documentation Standard

A multi-vehicle claim file documents which specific covered auto was involved, that vehicle's specific coverage selections from the Declarations page (Section 1), the driver's status (named insured, permitted household driver, or excluded driver per Sections 3–4), and confirmation of the correct Policy Form version in effect for that vehicle as of the date of loss (Section 6).
